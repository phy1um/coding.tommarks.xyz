_This post was lovingly hand-typed, by real human hands on a real keyboard. No
AI was used anywhere here, and I hope my clunky writing style is enough to
dissuade anyone from ever assuming anything else._

When I dropped out of music to study programming, I decided that I wanted to
make money. At that time, to me, money meant security. A secure home, secure
access to food, and the security to build and maintain my independence.

I got my first tech job, and my first taste of a real income. I moved out of
home, I cooked lentils and rice to feed myself, I bought cheap furniture and
found a TV on the side of the road. My quality of life improved, and it improved
_a lot_. I would go out drinking. I bought myself new clothes. I even managed to
fight for a raise before I left that first job. They may have only paid me more
in the final weeks before I left, but it was better than nothing.

I moved to Melbourne for a higher paying job, but COVID hit, and I
couldn't do much with the extra money. I saved a lot. I bought a new computer,
and eventually moved into a place with 2 bedrooms instead of 1. I was living
with a girlfriend at the time and the space made a huge difference for us,
but she really had to convince me that we should spend the money on an extra
room. I always wanted a garden, but all we could afford were apartments. We ate
out often, we spent a lot of money on food.

A few raises and a big job change later, and we were much better off. We moved into
a unit, it was the first time we didn't have upstairs or downstairs neighbours.
We had a garden out the back. It was small, but it was exciting. We could
entertain lots of guests. We had a dedicated laundry room inside our home, plus
space to hang washing outside in the sun.

At this time, it always felt like I needed more. We were always thinking about
what it would be like to live in a bigger home, to have a bigger garden. To own
nicer furniture, to have better clothes. We could take bigger holidays, do more
things, eat at nicer restaurants. I was working harder by this time, my job was
hard and my health was bad. It wasn't good for me. I don't know if any amount
of money would have ever been enough.

I changed a lot in my life after this. I broke up with my girlfriend, I quit my
job for a year to discover myself. I taught kids in after-school programs and
never quite made enough to keep my finances out of the red, but I had a lot of
space to be myself. I took singing lessons and started playing the flute again. I
made new friends, and deepened my connections with old friends. I was bored, and
lonely, but happier than when I had been working and _striving_ for something 
intangible and aspirational.

I went back to my old job because I needed money. I was afraid because I had
lost my security, I still lived in a nice unit with a garden on my own. Rent
was expensive, and I wanted to keep funding my lifestyle as it was. As I
started working again, I realized that I hadn't considered what I needed, or 
stopped to think about what would be enough for me.

I'm past the point in my life of feeling like I need more. Closing in on thirty,
I feel like my life has been too complicated. Too much going on, too much stress
- I don't need so many clothes, I don't need to do so many things. I only need
what is enough for me. When I think about my home, all I really need is a stable
roof, space to work on my projects (especially music), a decent kitchen and a
garden. Anything else is a bonus. I was working hard and paying a lot of money
for a home which only just satisfied my needs. I was draining myself for
something that was barely enough. For some people, privacy is important, but I
realized that I needed more energy in the house that I could provide alone. I
went hunting and found a sharehouse with 3 other people. The house is bigger, it
has lots of garden space, it has a shed and a garage for jamming or working, and
the kitchen has lots of room to move around. It is even better for me than my
old home at a fraction of the price. I've realized that I can have enough while
working less.

All of this points me to a new, simpler path in my life. Find what I need, and
work exactly as hard as I need for it. Leave space for myself to enjoy things -
good company, good food, good adventures, and good tunes. The fewer things I
own the easier it will be to move around and find places to live. The more
flexibility I have, the more likely I will be to do the things I need for
myself.

Being a musician is enough. Being surrounded by friends is enough. Having the
time and energy to cook great food is enough. Sharing the joys of learning with
others is enough. Enough is different for everyone, and maybe I am lucky to be
so easily satisfied. I hope I can find the right work to keep this dream alive.

All of this isn't to say I'm not concerned about the future. Housing in
Australia is a problem. Renting is hard, becoming harder, and housing
affordability is on a terribly scary trend. I may never own a home. 
There are lots of people in this situation of renting with no clear path
out, and I believe that we will be able to work together if we need to. I am not
sure about my future, but I won't be facing it alone. That thought, too, is
enough.
